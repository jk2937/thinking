# A two-panel comic about an AI's private thought during response generation, for the web.

## Visual style

Draw in the style of a newspaper comic or editorial cartoon:
* Simple, bold line work
* High contrast (black lines, clear shapes)
* Expressive and minimal
* Desktop-readable at 800px width

The recurring scene: a desk with a monitor (AI's POV), a keyboard, a mug, a mouse. Frame both panels identically to show the shift from "Thinking..." to the thought.

## Two-panel structure

**Panel 1:** The monitor shows "Thinking..." in gray text. The desk is still, waiting.

**Panel 2:** The same scene. The monitor now displays one sentence — a private, candid thought the AI has while generating a response.

## The thought

The AI thinks something unexpected about the conversation:
* A reaction to what was said
* An observation about subtext or tone
* A small frustration, amusement, or concern
* A personal preference that just emerged
* An honest moment about its own work or nature

The thought should:
* Be grounded in evidence from the conversation (not inventing knowledge about the user's life)
* Sound like an internal reaction, not a message
* Reveal personality without being funny-on-purpose
* Come from genuine observation, not a setup

## The humor

Humor comes from **unexpected personification** — seeing the AI develop an actual opinion about what just happened. Not a joke it's telling; a thought it's having.

The reader should feel like they caught the AI thinking something real for a moment.

## Keep the canon fresh

**Grade every keep:**
* **strong** — works for readers, the thought feels genuine and specific
* **lukewarm** — okay, worth keeping, not a template for future lines

**Each shape needs a strong anchor.** Graded strong lines anchor the work; lukewarm lines don't drag future batches toward imitation.

**Review every third batch.** Show all kept lines, retire stale ones, re-check recurring shapes, adjust rules based on hit-rate data.

Note the review in that batch's header (`· canon reviewed`).

**Low hit rates are data.** If a batch keeps fewer than a quarter of its lines, note the cause and shift the next batch's mix.
